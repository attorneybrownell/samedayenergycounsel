Questions

Do I pay anything upfront?

No. There is no retainer and no advance fee — and no payment to request a quote. The deliverables are sent first, and the invoice — with a LawPay payment link — arrives with them.

How does pricing work now?

Every review is quoted per ask. Send the document and you get a firm flat-fee quote — for the initial review and for subsequent turns — within 2 business hours. Two flagships publish starting-at prices so you can budget: lease review from $750 for the first turn (from $250 per additional turn) and EPC review from $2,500 (from $1,000 per additional turn). Your quote confirms the final number for your document before any work starts; if you don’t accept it, nothing is owed.

How fast is the quote — and in what hours?

Within 2 business hours of a complete request. Business hours are counted on business days (Monday–Friday, excluding federal holidays); a request that arrives outside them is quoted within the first 2 business hours of the next business day.

What exactly is a “turn”?

One review pass on one draft of one document. On the flagships, lease turns start at $750 for the first turn and $250 for each further pass; EPC turns start at $2,500 and $1,000. Other menu items are quoted per ask for the initial review and each subsequent turn. A materially different document — a different lease, contract, or property — starts a new first turn.

When does the one-business-day clock start?

When two things have both happened: you accept the quote, and the intake inputs are complete. Accepted by 12:00 p.m. Central on a business day means delivery by 5:00 p.m. Central that day; after noon, or on a weekend or federal holiday, it means 5:00 p.m. the next business day. The clock does not start at your initial request or while a quote is outstanding. If an input is missing, you are told promptly what is needed.

What happens if a deadline is missed?

You are told before the deadline passes, given a new committed time, and that turn is discounted 30% off the quoted fee — for example, a $750 first turn becomes $525 and a $250 additional turn becomes $175 on the lease bundle; a $2,500 EPC first turn becomes $1,750 and a $1,000 additional turn becomes $700.

Is a lawyer actually doing this work, or is it just AI?

Both, deliberately divided. AI-assisted systems do the legwork — clause extraction, document comparison, table-building, formatting, deck drafting. The legal judgment — the positions taken, the fallbacks, what counts as a red flag — is the lawyer’s, and every deliverable is reviewed and signed off by counsel before it goes out. That division is exactly why a review can be quoted as a firm flat fee instead of an open-ended hourly bill.

What is the Board & CEO FAQ in each bundle?

It is the sixth deliverable in both the Lease Review Bundle and the EPC Contract Review Bundle — a short, plain-language Q&A about your own document, written for the CEO or board member who has to approve the deal, not for the negotiation call. It answers the questions leadership asks first: total exposure, what decisions are needed and by when, what delay costs, what exit would cost, and counsel’s recommendation. It is drawn from the redline and the rest of the review, so it adds no extra fee and no extra turnaround time.

How is a wind lease review different from a solar one?

The core is the same — term, rent, renewal, assignment, restoration. Solar adds roof condition and access, shading, and interconnection questions. Wind adds surface-use conflicts, access roads and setbacks, and how the wind rights sit against mineral rights and existing surface uses. The redline branches accordingly.
